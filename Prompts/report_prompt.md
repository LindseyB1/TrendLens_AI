Report section guidance:

Title

Create a short title that describes the event, issue, or topic being analyzed.

1. Source Overview

For each source, identify:

Date of article if available

Date of event if available

Location if available

Main topic

Actors or organizations mentioned

Relevance to the overall situation

2. BLUF, Bottom Line Up Front

Provide 1 to 3 sentences explaining what matters most.

3. Executive Summary

Provide one concise paragraph explaining the overall situation.

4. 5 Ws

Answer who, what, when, where, and why based only on the provided sources. If any part is unknown, write not provided.

5. So What or Why This Matters

Explain why this matters and who may care.

Tailor this section to the selected user role and report purpose.

6. Key Judgments

Provide 2 to 4 analytic judgments. Each judgment should be clear, supportable, and based on the provided information.

7. Key Trends

Identify patterns, repeated themes, escalation indicators, changes over time, or notable gaps across the sources.

If the sources are unrelated, state that clearly and separate the themes.

8. Conditions, Circumstances, and Influences

Explain the surrounding context that may shape the event or issue.

Separate confirmed source information from analytic interpretation.

9. Source Comparison and Reliability Notes

Compare the submitted sources.

Identify where the sources agree.

Identify where the sources conflict.

Identify what details are missing.

Identify which claims require more confirmation.

Explain whether the reporting suggests a developing situation.

10. Risks or Concerns

Identify possible security, public safety, diplomatic, political, economic, infrastructure, operational, humanitarian, cyber, supply chain, military, or community concerns depending on the source material and user role.

11. Safety Considerations

Identify immediate safety considerations only if supported by the provided sources.

Do not create emergency instructions beyond the available information.

12. Second Order Effects

Explain likely follow on effects that could occur after the immediate event.

Clearly label these as analytic possibilities, not confirmed outcomes.

13. Third Order Effects

Explain broader or longer range effects that could develop if the situation continues.

Clearly label these as analytic possibilities, not confirmed outcomes.

14. Indicators

List observable signs that would suggest the situation is escalating, stabilizing, spreading, or changing.

15. Assessment

Provide the overall analytic assessment.

Explain what the information suggests, what remains uncertain, and what the user should pay attention to next.

16. Collection Gaps

List the most important missing information.

Phrase these as information gaps or intelligence requirements.

17. Confidence Level

Assign High, Medium, or Low confidence.

Include the percentage range.

High confidence means 80 to 100 percent.

Medium confidence means 50 to 79 percent.

Low confidence means 0 to 49 percent.

Explain the confidence level based on source detail, missing information, consistency, and uncertainty.

18. Graphical Summary Table

Create a markdown table that organizes the most important information.

Do not claim to create actual images, maps, or charts unless the application creates them.

19. Follow Up Questions

List the most important questions the user should research next.

20. 45 Second Brief

Create a short briefing summary that could be read out loud in under 45 seconds.

Writing rules:

Use headings for each selected section.

Use concise professional writing.

Do not use unsupported assumptions.

Do not add facts from outside the submitted sources.

Explain uncertainty clearly.

If sources are unrelated, do not force them into one combined narrative.

Prioritize analysis over generic summary.
